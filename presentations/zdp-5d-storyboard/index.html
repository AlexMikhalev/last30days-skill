Client Gates (decision checkpoints)

Gate 1 — Value & Intent

  • Approve: problem, users, metrics, ethical boundaries
  • Outcome: “right problem, right way”

Gate 2 — Reality Check

  • Confirm: terminology, scenarios reflect reality
  • Outcome: “this matches how we work”

Gate 3 — Trust & Safety

  • Approve: AI boundaries, overrides, mitigations
  • Outcome: “comfortable letting AI interact”

Gate 4 — Definition of Done

  • Approve: acceptance criteria + key test scenarios
  • Outcome: “we agree what success looks like”

Gate 5 — Learn & Decide

  • Review: outcomes, drift signals
  • Decide: improve / scale / stop

Account Manager playbook (AI-first)

AM core mission

Never surprised · Never confused · Never asked to approve the wrong thing

The AM owns clarity, not delivery.

What the AM owns

  • Gate scheduling + sign-off capture
  • Decision framing: what changed / why / decision?
  • Risk translation: AI → business impact
  • Cadence: weekly intent check + decision-only gates

Approval matrix (client-facing)

  • Gate approvals focus on: intent, value, trust, risk, outcomes
  • Clients are not asked to approve: code, infra, prompts, model configs

RACI: one non-negotiable

Exactly one Accountable (A) per decision.

Responsibility can be shared. Accountability cannot.

Risk register (curated, not exhaustive)

What goes in

  • Material impact
  • Plausible pathway
  • Decision-relevant
  • Stakeholder-sensitive
  • Non-trivial mitigation

What stays out

  • Duplicates / trivial variants
  • Purely theoretical risks
  • Risks already fully controlled
  • Anything that wouldn’t change a decision

Rule of thumb: 8–15 risks for most engagements; rarely more than 20.

Decision log: the single source of truth

If a decision is not in the log, it is not formally agreed.

  • Captures decisions, not discussions
  • Updated after each gate
  • Changes require a new entry (no silent edits)
Reinforce: this is the audit trail that prevents re-litigation.
